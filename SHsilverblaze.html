SILVER BLAZE


Holmes told Watson that he has to go to Dartmoor due to the disappearance of the favorite horse of the Wessex Cup and the tragic murder of its trainer John Straker. Colonel Ross was the Silver Blaze, the favorite's owner. Inspector Gregory is looking after this case; who invited Mr Holmes's cooperation. John Straker owned an establishment and three lads of whom one used to sit overnight accompanied by a dog to look after, while the other two (light-sleepers) slept in the loft.

At night, about 1'o clock, the two lads walked up to the trainer's house while their maid took the supper of curried mutton for the third lad at the stable. Unexpectedly, the maid was told to stop by a stranger and asked at which place they were then and also said about the trainer's stable. The frightened maid rushed to stable to hand over the meal and the stranger was close to her heels. At the very night, a tragedy took place where the third lad was drugged, the favorite disappeared and Straker murdered.

Near his body, a small cataract knife with his blood and cravat was found. The cravat belonged to Fitzroy Simpson who registered a huge amount against the favorite. Afterwards, Silas Brown accepted that the stranger was he himself but denied any sinister motive. Holmes listened to the Inspector's supposition and then inspected the Straker's belongings. There were bill, candle, a box of vestas etc. The bill showed heavy purchases from milliner under the name Madame Derbyshire. Holmes inquired to the trainer's wife about the bill where she answered that it were his friend's. Holmes caught the sight of small candle in the mud and his trainer's coat neatly kept on bush. Holmes noticed that the sheeps in trainer's farm were lame. Holmes and Watson later followed the trail which led to Mapleton stables, of Silas Brown. Holmes talked with him and then returned.

Holmes assured Colonel Ross that Silver Blaze would run for the Wessex Cup and it happened as he had promised. Holmes later revealed that Silver Blaze was the murderer of John Straker. John had a second wife(Madame Derbyshire) for whom heavy purchases were done and hence, was plunged in debt. Apparently, to gain capital, he decided to make his horse lose. He had drugged the supper with opium, drug resembling to the taste of curry. Except the family who would know about the meal to be cooked the same day. Even the dog in the stable didn't bark because trainer was familiar. Also, he had practised tearing tendons of the sheep before the horse. That night, he took his horse away from the stables to tear his tendons so that the favorite would lose. As soon as he struck the match , with a sudden glare, the horse impromptu struck him hard due to animal instinct. Also, the cataract knife wounded due to the horse's attack and the horse started running which was found later at the Mapleton's. That's how bankrupted John Straker's plan spoiled.
